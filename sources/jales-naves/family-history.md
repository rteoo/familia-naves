# Family history — consolidated editorial notes

Collected 2026-11-11. All linked editorial sources are hosted by Editora Naves. Dates described as approximate remain approximate. Original archival images were not independently authenticated.

## Earliest Brazilian generation

[Jales, 2025-05-22](https://editoranaves.com.br/a-familia-naves-comecou-sua-historia-na-vila-de-sao-paulo-em-1653-e-foi-para-minas-gerai/) · [local record](catalogue/2025.md#post-12810)

This 2025 overview places João de Almeida Naves's arrival around 1650, marriage to Maria da Silva Leite probably in São Paulo in 1653, and subsequent settlement in Santana de Parnaíba. It reports baptism in Portugal on 1624-12-17, parental marriage on 1622-12-04 and João's death on 1715-03-11. It gives ten children. The printed 1969/1975 dates are retained as source errors; the cross-check below supports 1669/1675 for the consolidated account.

[Jales, 2025-05-22](https://editoranaves.com.br/guerra-entre-familias-na-vila-de-sp-levou-os-naves-a-se-mudarem-para-santana-de-parnaiba/) · [local record](catalogue/2025.md#post-12818)

This companion article associates the move with the Pires–Camargo conflict. It dates Baltazar's baptism to 1654-05-06 and João's to 1655, while calling the 1650 arrival and 1653 marriage probable. The printed 1973 date for Maria is implausible; the sources below instead support 1669. A historical narrative is not equivalent to a directly inspected parish record.

## Research changed the child count

[Jales, 2017-04-06](https://editoranaves.com.br/inventario-de-joao-de-almeida-naves/) · [local record](catalogue/2017.md#post-1171)

The 2017 inventory article reports eight children and explains the difficulties of interpreting a typed certificate of the 1715 estate record. It points to Notícias de Naves, issue 204, dated 2011-03-03. This is a lead to documentation, not our own archival reading.

[Jales, 2018-08-28](https://editoranaves.com.br/nilson-amplia-pesquisa-sobre-familia-naves/) · [local record](catalogue/2018.md#post-7410)

The 2018 research update explicitly expands the reported child count from eight to ten following discoveries by Nilson N. Naves, including Baltasar and João. It reports baptism dates 1654-05-06 and 1655-10-23 and an approximate 1653 marriage. That explains why older counts should not automatically override later research.

[Jales, 2017-10-10](https://editoranaves.com.br/familia-naves-brasileira/) · [local record](catalogue/2017.md#post-2111)

The 2017 general overview separately gives seven children, a 1655 marriage and Maria Januária as Américo Brasiliense's wife. Its statement that Francisco de Paula was born in 1933 conflicts with its own much earlier descendants. These claims are retained as source variants, not normalized into the current tree.

## Florência and the Minas Gerais descendants

[Jales, 2025-07-06](https://editoranaves.com.br/florencia-naves-deu-sequencia-a-familia-no-brasi/) · [local record](catalogue/2025.md#post-12998)

The July 2025 article links Florência da Silva Naves and Domingos Lopes da Silva to their sole son João de Almeida Naves, who married Luzia Moreira de Afonseca. It explicitly treats Florência's birth, marriage and her son's birth as uncertain (the latter approximately 1715). It reports eight children of João and Luzia, including João Naves Damasceno, and dates Damasceno's death to 1831-05-30 and marriage to Anna Victoria de São Thomé to 1786-02-24. It reports Anna's death as 1841-05-19. These are editorially reported dates requiring original records for definitive proof.

## Damasceno's estate record

[Jales, 2025-07-09](https://editoranaves.com.br/inventario-de-joao-naves-damasceno-e-o-trabalho-em-equipe/) · [local record](catalogue/2025.md#post-13012)

Jales describes Nilson's discovery of the inventory, Abilon's support obtaining it, and Edriana Aparecida Nolasco's transcription. The reported archival reference is inventory year 1832, box 563, at Museu Regional de São João del Rei. Its opening is dated 1832-07-05 and the death to 1831-05-30. The reproduced condensed heir list contains eleven people; this should not be mistaken automatically for the total lifetime number of children. Other articles report twelve children.

The article points to Notícias de Naves issue 86 (2007-10-09) and page 54 of the family history book. These are documentary leads; neither the bulletin nor book was collected in this run.

## Route toward Messias and Anésia

[Jales, 2025-11-29](https://editoranaves.com.br/haroldo-naves-falara-no-encontro-da-familia-sobre-11-geracoes/) · [local record](catalogue/2025.md#post-13587)

The November 2025 account connects Manoel Antônio Naves and Anna Rosa Sampaio da Silva to Umbelina Rosa Naves; and Maria Rita Naves and Alexandre Esmayel de Carvalho to Francisco de Paula Naves. Umbelina and Francisco are reported as parents of Américo Brasiliense Naves. The source names Américo's wife as Maria Cândida de Mello and their son as Messias Brasiliense Naves. Messias and Maria José Pereira are parents of six children, including Pedro Silvério; Anésia's sibling relationship is corroborated in the Quirinópolis sources. The conflicting spouse name in other Jales articles remains unresolved.

The same source describes eleven generations in Haroldo's branch. Its generational numbering belongs to that stated lineage and should not be applied to unrelated branches without checking each parent-child link.

## Haroldo Naves — institutional cross-check

Checked on 2026-11-11; these official references supplement Jales for the specific discrepancies requested by the user.

- **Campos Verdes mayor:** the [municipality's historical chronology](https://camposverdes.go.gov.br/nossa-historia/) lists **2001–2004, 2017–2020 and 2021–2024**: three terms, rather than the four stated in Jales's 2024 article.
- **FGM presidency:** [Alego's report of the 2023-05-23 inauguration](https://portal.al.go.leg.br/noticias/133301/posse-da-nova-diretoria-da-fgm) explicitly identifies **2023–2025 as his fourth mandate** and his first assumption as 2017. The [FGM's 2025 transition report](https://www.fgm-go.org.br/Imprensa/Noticias/Paulo-vitor-avelar-toma-posse-como-novo-presidente-da-fgm-em-cerimonia-no-palacio-das-esmeraldas-118/) instead says two mandates. Preserve the institutional disagreement; use the explicit fourth-mandate inauguration as evidence for the count, without inventing all cycle boundaries.
- **Sebrae award:** the [Senate Annals for March 2003, printed page 468](https://www.senado.leg.br/publicacoes/anais/pdf-digitalizado/Anais_Republica/2003/2003%20Livro%205.pdf) identifies **base year 2002** and **Centro-Oeste regional winner**. The [Senate biographical index, printed page 79](https://www2.senado.leg.br/bdsf/bitstream/handle/id/242335/Dados%20Biograficos%20Senadoras%20Vol%20II.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2) points to the report of delivery in DSF 2003-03-27, page 4850. Thus 2002 and the 2003 reporting year are different labels, not grounds to declare one a simple typo. The precise ceremony date was not independently established.

The [Sebrae retrospective](https://sebrae.com.br/Sebrae/Portal%20Sebrae/Arquivos/Livro%207%C2%AA%20Edi%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20PSPE.pdf) is indexed as identifying Haroldo as the Centro-Oeste winner of edition II, but the PDF did not open reliably in this research. Treat that component as indexed evidence. The municipal history's national-level wording does not establish that he was the sole national champion.

The [FGM historical list](https://www.fgm-go.org.br/A-Federacao/Conheca-FGM/) reports an interruption in 2020–2021 and contains an internally inconsistent 2001–2025 label for Haroldo. Do not silently repair it or describe his presidency as uninterrupted from 2017 to 2025. Full cycle dates remain unverified. Some direct page fetches timed out; Alego's relevant text and the oversized Senate Annals were inspected through their indexed primary-source content, with the municipal chronology read directly.
